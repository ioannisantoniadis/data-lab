# Pilot results: data-lab and rl-for-llms (protocol v1, 2026-10-05)

Two blinded copies per book, four independent fresh-agent auditors, the same 30-item sample
for both auditors of a book, four planted errors per book (two inside the sample, two outside).
Auditor reports were returned as text and saved by the orchestrator; non-planted defects were
checked against the original repositories before being counted.

## Results

| Measure | data-lab | rl-for-llms |
|---|---|---|
| Book size (prose words, approx.) | 26,000 | 32,000 |
| Sampled items with a checkable statement | 24–26 of 30 | 25–26 of 30 |
| Sample defect rate, pooled (wrong or unsupported, plants excluded) | 1/26 = 4% [1%, 19%] | 1/26 = 4% [1%, 19%] |
| Planted errors found (auditor A / B) | 4/4, 3/4 | 4/4, 4/4 |
| Kappa between auditors (defect vs not) | 0.00 (one disagreement, base rate near zero) | 1.00 |
| Real defects found in the full read, major | 0 | 0 |
| Real defects found in the full read, minor | 8 | 12 |
| Minor defects per 10,000 words | about 3 | about 4 |
| Reproduction from a clean copy | all steps; outputs bit-identical | all steps; outputs identical in content |
| Quoted numbers generated by scripts | about 44% (chapters 8–15) | 0% (all typed; about 96% of traced ones match script output) |
| Sampled checkable claims naming a test | about 50% | 10–19% |

Planting sensitivity overall: 15 of 16 (94%); inside the sample 8 of 8, outside 7 of 8 (the
one miss was a factor error in an equation outside the sample).

**Real defects, data-lab:** a misquoted Sorscher et al. phrase cited to the wrong place (the
abstract instead of §1; introduced by the final-audit fixes); a caption stating "two clusters
of 20" where 20 is the total; a published value rendering as "0.050 positives"; the classifier
two-sample test's squared features not described; a test weaker than its stated bound; a
figure-script docstring saying median for mean; an unrecorded "about a third" result; a bound
loose by one.

**Real defects, rl-for-llms:** Christiano et al. grouped with the PPO recipe; "three rarest
tasks" where four fall to zero; a hard-coded "85% correct" that one auditor recomputed as 64% (not yet confirmed); "every baselined
estimator beats REINFORCE at every G" false at G=2; a "medium" prompt that is the easiest;
0.18 for 0.19; three near-verbatim quotes; a DPO end distance differing between chapters
without explanation; symbol reuse against the book's own notation rule; edit links missing
`repo-subdir`; bibliography key/year mismatches; an unsupported "dominate" claim.

## What the pilot shows

1. **The two books are not distinguishable on correctness at this sample size.** Both sample
   defect rates are 4% with intervals of about 1–19%, and the full-read densities (about 3 vs
   about 4 minor defects per 10,000 words, no major ones in either) are within noise.
2. **data-lab's extra structure shows up as verifiability, not as fewer errors.** More of its
   numbers are generated and more of its claims are tested, so an error is more likely to be
   caught by a machine; rl-for-llms's typed numbers matched its scripts almost as often. The
   most recent data-lab error was introduced by a process step (an audit fix).
3. **The auditors are sensitive:** 94% of planted errors found, all inside the sample. A clean
   result from this method means something.

## Protocol changes for v2

1. **Sample size.** At 30 items the rate cannot separate books. Use the full-read defect
   density (confirmed defects per 10,000 words, by severity) as the primary metric, and a
   sample of 100 items when a rate is wanted.
2. **Classifier.** 3–4 of 30 sampled items had no checkable statement; require a verb of
   assertion or a number tied to a result, and drop cross-reference-only sentences.
3. **Blinding must not break the build.** Keep the README's original build commands (the
   neutral README dropped rl-for-llms's `--extra` flags, which one auditor reported as a
   defect), and keep files that tests read (data-lab's `COVERAGE.md`), or mark those tests.
4. **Reports come back as text.** Subagents cannot write report files; the brief should ask
   for the report in the final message, and give each auditor its own work directory (two
   wrote scratch files outside their copies).
5. **Adjudication step.** The orchestrator checks every non-planted defect against the
   original before it is counted (done here by hand), and resolves disagreements (one item).

## Merge-readiness: notation clashes found across the two books

- **β:** learning-curve exponent (data-lab) vs KL strength and DPO temperature (rl-for-llms).
- **α:** Zipf exponent vs Hoffmann's exponent vs mixup's Beta parameter (data-lab).
- **λ:** Box–Cox (data-lab) vs GAE, ORPO, KTO and RLCR coefficients (rl-for-llms).
- **γ:** discount vs SimPO margin vs PPO-ptx coefficient (rl-for-llms).
- **q:** sampling distribution (both) vs a success probability and a confidence (rl-for-llms).
- **r:** reward vs a probability ratio (rl-for-llms).
- **D, E, N, T, s, m:** dataset/tokens, learning curve/irreducible loss, dimension/parameters,
  transform/mask/teacher, selection probability/α+1, draws/m(x) (data-lab).
- **π:** a policy in rl-for-llms, a permutation in loss-functions-lab.

Shared devices that already align: `callout-tip` cards with eight fixed fields generated from
a records file (data cards; method cards), `callout-important` lineage boxes, evidence labels
with "as of" dates, one script per figure with a shared theme. The chapter templates differ
slightly (data-lab adds "What the toy cannot show" per chapter).
