| Technique | Lever | Assumption | Fit on | Failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Stratified sampling](01-the-data-generating-process.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | Strata are known for the whole population before sampling, and the target varies more between strata than within them | The population frame: stratum sizes | Strata unrelated to the target (no gain); disproportionate allocation without weights (biased estimates) |
| [Finding label errors by confident learning](02-labels.qmd) | Labels | Class-conditional noise (flips independent of $x$ given the true class); out-of-sample probabilities that rank examples well; classes that overlap little | Out-of-sample predictions (cross-validation), never the model's own training fit | Overlapping classes: genuine ambiguity looks like noise (precision near the noise rate); flips near the boundary are missed; noise that depends on $x$ breaks the assumption |
| [Spectrogram (short-time Fourier magnitude)](03-data-types.qmd) | Representation | The task depends on which frequencies are present and when, not on their phase; the window is long enough to resolve the frequencies that matter | None: a fixed transform (window length and overlap chosen in advance) | Window too short to resolve close frequencies or too long to localize changes in time; aliasing of frequencies above half the sampling rate happens before the transform and cannot be undone |
| [Time-ordered evaluation splits](03-data-types.qmd) | Evaluation only | Predictions will be made about later times than the training data; the series is autocorrelated or drifts | Split boundaries set by time stamps | Features computed over the whole series (rolling statistics, normalizers) still carry the future into the past; a single future window gives a noisy estimate |
| [Complete-case analysis (dropping rows with missing values)](04-cleaning.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | MCAR: whether a value is missing is unrelated to anything | Nothing is fit; the selection is made by the missingness itself | MAR or MNAR missingness (biased estimates); many columns with a little missingness each (few complete rows left) |
| [Mean imputation](04-cleaning.qmd) | Representation | MCAR, and only the column's mean matters downstream | Training rows only (the observed mean) | Non-MCAR missingness; heavy missingness (variance collapses); downstream use of variances or correlations |
| [Regression imputation](04-cleaning.qmd) | Representation | MAR on the observed columns used as predictors, and a correctly specified imputation model | Training rows with the value observed | MNAR (the missing values differ in ways the predictors do not show); treating imputed values as observed |
| [Removing train/test duplicates](04-cleaning.qmd) | Evaluation only | Deployment inputs will not be copies of training inputs | Exact (or near-duplicate) matching of test rows against training rows | Near-duplicates that exact matching misses; deployment that genuinely repeats inputs, where duplicates are part of $p$ |
| [Fitting preprocessing on training data only](04-cleaning.qmd) | Evaluation only | The evaluation should mimic deployment, where test labels and test inputs are not available at fit time | Training folds only (inside each cross-validation fold) | Steps fit outside the pipeline (feature selection, target encoding, resampling) before splitting |
| [Affine scaling (standard, min-max, max-abs)](05-affine-scaling.qmd) | Representation | The model's behavior depends on the features' units; a feature's typical spread is a fair yardstick for its importance | Training data only: mean and standard deviation, or minimum and maximum, or maximum absolute value | Outliers set the scale (standard and min-max); a feature that should matter more is made to matter equally; test values outside the training range (min-max) |
| [Robust scaling (median and interquartile range)](05-affine-scaling.qmd) | Representation | Outliers are a small share of each feature (fewer than a quarter on each side) | Training data only: median and quartiles | Outliers are left in place at large values; features with an interquartile range of zero (mostly constant) |
| [Log-transforming a skewed target](06-changing-the-shape.qmd) | Representation | $y > 0$, with multiplicative noise or right skew | None (a fixed function); the smearing correction is fit on training residuals | Retransformation bias; zeros and negatives; smearing fails when the log-scale noise depends on $x$, and its factor is unstable when residuals are heavy-tailed |
| [Box-Cox and Yeo-Johnson power transforms](06-changing-the-shape.qmd) | Representation | Some power of the data is close to normal; Box-Cox needs strictly positive data, Yeo-Johnson does not | Training data only: $\lambda$ by maximum likelihood | No power makes the data normal (multimodal data); $\lambda$ estimated on few points is noisy; retransformation bias as for the log |
| [Quantile transform](06-changing-the-shape.qmd) | Representation | Only the order of values within a feature matters, not their spacing | Training data only: the quantiles of each feature | Distances lose their meaning (two values far apart in the tail become neighbors); no extrapolation: everything beyond the training range maps to the boundary |
| [One-hot and ordinal encoding](07-encoding-and-features.qmd) | Representation | One-hot: none about order; ordinal: the levels are ordered and the code's spacing means something to the model | Training data only: the list of levels | Ordinal codes for unordered categories; unseen levels at test time; one-hot with very many levels (wide, sparse, rare columns) |
| [Feature hashing](07-encoding-and-features.qmd) | Representation | Collisions between levels are rare enough, or harmless enough, to accept for fixed memory | Nothing: the hash is fixed | Collisions grow with the number of levels: 2,000 levels in 1,024 columns use only about 880 of them; hashed columns cannot be traced back to levels |
| [Target encoding](07-encoding-and-features.qmd) | Representation | Levels with few rows are shrunk toward the global mean; each row's encoding is computed without that row's own label (cross-fitting) | Training labels, cross-fitted on the training rows (out-of-fold), and fit on all training rows for test data | Encoding training rows with an encoder fit on those same rows: noise becomes a training signal that fails on new data; leakage across a time order |
| [Splines and discretization](07-encoding-and-features.qmd) | Representation | The effect of $x$ is smooth (splines) or roughly constant within intervals (bins); enough data per knot or bin | Training data only: knot or bin positions (quantiles or a uniform grid) | Too many knots or bins (variance); extrapolation beyond the training range; bins hide variation within an interval |
| [TF-IDF](07-encoding-and-features.qmd) | Representation | Word order does not matter for the task; terms common to every document carry little information | Training documents only: the vocabulary and document frequencies | Order and context are lost; rare terms are noisy; the vocabulary is fixed at fit time; the default token pattern drops one-character tokens |
| [Rebalancing classes (undersampling or oversampling)](08-sampling-and-weighting.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | Only the class priors change: $q(x \mid y) = p(x \mid y)$ | Training labels (class counts) | Probabilities are wrong unless corrected; undersampling discards data and raises variance; oversampling by copying repeats examples |
| [Class weights](08-sampling-and-weighting.qmd) | Weights $w$ | As for rebalancing: only the class priors are to be changed | Training labels (class counts); scikit-learn's "balanced" uses n / (classes x count) | Probabilities are wrong unless corrected; very large weights on very rare classes make training noisy |
| [Prior-shift correction (and EM prior estimation)](08-sampling-and-weighting.qmd) | Weights $w$ | Only the priors differ between training and target data; the model's posteriors are calibrated under $q$; for EM, the new data are unlabeled draws from the target | The training prior (counts) and the target prior (known, or estimated by EM on unlabeled target data) | $p(x \mid y)$ also changed (not prior shift); miscalibrated training posteriors; EM converging slowly or to a biased value with few target examples |
| [Importance weighting for covariate shift](08-sampling-and-weighting.qmd) | Weights $w$ | Covariate shift: $p(y \mid x) = q(y \mid x)$; $q(x) > 0$ wherever $p(x) > 0$; the density ratio is known or well estimated | The density ratio: known here; in practice estimated, which adds its own error | Variance grows with $\mathbb{E}_q[w^2]$, which grows exponentially with a Gaussian shift; regions where $q$ is tiny dominate; an estimated ratio can be badly wrong where data is sparse |
| [SMOTE (synthetic minority oversampling)](08-sampling-and-weighting.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | The minority class occupies the space between nearby minority examples (locally convex); features are on comparable scales | Training minority examples only, inside each training fold | Minority clusters smaller than $k$, or separated by majority regions: synthetic points land between clusters; noisy minority labels are interpolated; applied before the split it leaks |
| [Label-preserving augmentation (flips, crops, color, SpecAugment, RandAugment)](09-augmentation.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | The target is invariant under the transform: $p(y \mid T(x)) = p(y \mid x)$ for every transform $T$ used, at the strengths used | Training data only, transformed during training | The invariance is false, or false beyond some strength: augmented examples are then mislabeled; test-time inputs never look like the augmented ones |
| [Label-changing augmentation](09-augmentation.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$, Labels | The transform's effect on the label is known exactly (here: a left-right flip reverses which half holds more ink) | Training data only | A wrongly assumed label change is label noise by construction; the effect may be known only for some inputs |
| [mixup](09-augmentation.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$, Labels | Between two training examples, the target changes linearly along the segment joining them (a vicinal distribution around the data) | Training batches, with the mixing weight drawn from Beta($\alpha$, $\alpha$) | Mixed inputs that are not plausible inputs; targets that are not linear between examples; small $\alpha$ changes little, large $\alpha$ blurs classes |
| [Detecting shift with two-sample tests](10-distribution-shift.qmd) | Evaluation only | Samples within each period are i.i.d.; for input-only tests, the shift that matters shows up in $p(x)$ | A reference sample from training time; for the classifier test, half of each sample (the other half is held out) | Concept shift leaves the inputs unchanged and is invisible to input-only tests; small shifts need large samples; a detected shift may not hurt the model, and an undetected one may |
| [Estimating data needs from a pilot learning curve](11-learning-curves.qmd) | Evaluation only | The fitted form holds beyond the pilot range; the pilot range already shows the asymptotic behavior; the floor $C$ is identifiable from the pilot | The pilot data only, with train/test splits that never put copies of a row on both sides (bootstrap included) | The floor is poorly determined, so targets near it are often judged unreachable; the pre-asymptotic curve is steeper than the asymptote, so needed sizes are underestimated; intervals are wide |
| [Coverage-driven selection](13-why-power-laws.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | What counts as covered can be recognized; unlabeled draws are cheap relative to labels or training steps | An unlabeled pool (or an oracle) and the record of what has been labeled | The gain is per label: it costs about $n^{1+\alpha}$ unlabeled draws, and per draw nothing beats uniform sampling; a pool proportional to the labeling budget gives only a constant-factor gain; recognizing coverage is trivial here and hard for real data (near-duplicates, semantic similarity) |
| [Pruning by difficulty (keep hard or easy examples)](14-choosing-data.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | The score ranks examples as the true margin would; the right end to keep depends on how much data there is | Scores from a probe model trained on the training data (a few epochs, or a different architecture) | Keeping hard examples with scarce data; noisy scores at high pruning rates, where random pruning can win (Ayed and Hayou); hard examples that are mislabeled |
| [Deduplicating training data](14-choosing-data.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$, Weights $w$ | Repeats are artifacts of collection, not part of $p$; what counts as a repeat (exact or near) can be defined | The whole corpus, before any split | Near-duplicates missed by exact matching; deduplicating genuine frequency information away; deduplicating after splitting |
| [Active learning by uncertainty sampling](14-choosing-data.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | Uncertainty reflects what the model has not learned, not irreducible label noise; the pool represents $p$ | The labeled set so far and an unlabeled pool | Noisy labels: uncertainty points at noise, and the gain vanishes; the labeled set is no longer a sample of $p$ (biased estimates); querying outliers |
| [Training on model-generated data](14-choosing-data.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | The generator's distribution covers $p$, including its tail | The generator's own training data | Replacing real data generation after generation loses more of the tail each time; accumulating real and synthetic data does not |
| [RHO-LOSS (reducible holdout loss selection)](14-choosing-data.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | Holdout data from $p$ is available; a small model's loss estimates what is irreducible (noise) for each point | A holdout set, through the irreducible-loss model | A poor irreducible-loss model misjudges noise; holdout data that is not from $p$ |
| [Choosing domain mixtures (DoReMi)](14-choosing-data.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | Weights found by a small proxy model transfer to a larger model | A small proxy model trained with group distributionally robust optimization over domains | Weights that do not transfer to the larger model or to the target task |
| [Filtering web-scale data](14-choosing-data.qmd) | Sampling distribution $q$ | The filter's notion of quality predicts usefulness for the target; enough data remains for the compute available | Heuristics or classifiers, sometimes trained on reference data | Filtering away rare but needed content (the tail); filtering choices that are right for one compute budget and wrong for another (Goyal et al.) |
